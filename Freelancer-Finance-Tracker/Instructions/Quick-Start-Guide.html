Getting Started with the Spreadsheet

1

Open and Make a Copy

Google Sheets: Click the link provided, then go to File > Make a Copy. This creates your own editable version.
Excel: Download the .xlsx file and open it in Excel or compatible software. Enable editing when prompted.

2

Start with the Income Tracker

This is your foundation. Every time you receive a payment, log it here. Enter the date, source, gross amount, and your preferred tax rate (we recommend 25% to start). The formulas automatically calculate your tax set-aside and net income.

3

Log Your Expenses

Track every expense in the Expense Tracker tab. Categorize each entry and mark whether it is recurring and/or tax deductible. This data feeds directly into your Dashboard.

4

Set Up Your Monthly Budget

At the start of each month, fill in your expected income and budgeted amounts. As the month progresses, fill in your actuals. The sheet calculates differences and tells you if you are on track.

5

Review Your Dashboard

The Dashboard automatically pulls data from all other tabs. It shows your monthly profit, income by source, expense breakdown, savings progress, and debt status -- all in one view. Check it weekly.

Important: Do not rename the tabs in the spreadsheet. The formulas reference tab names to pull data across sheets. If you rename a tab, the cross-sheet formulas will break.

Your 7 Spreadsheet Tabs Explained

1. Dashboard

Your command center. Shows monthly profit, income vs. expenses, savings progress, and debt status. All data pulls automatically from other tabs.

2. Income Tracker

Log every payment with date, source, gross amount, and tax rate. Automatically calculates tax set-aside and net income. Includes summary by source.

3. Expense Tracker

Record all spending with categories, payment methods, and deductible flags. Auto-calculates totals by category and identifies tax-deductible expenses.

4. Monthly Budget

Plan your month with expected vs. actual columns. Includes fixed expenses with due dates and checkboxes, variable expenses, and a health check section.

5. Tax Reserve Calculator

Tracks quarterly estimated taxes. Shows both 25% and 30% reserve calculations. Includes a deductions checklist and tax savings account tracker.

6. Debt Payoff Tracker

Uses the Debt Snowball method. Auto-sorts by smallest balance. Shows total payment, estimated payoff dates, and snowball rollover strategy.

7. Savings Tracker

Set up to 5 savings goals with targets, deadlines, and progress tracking. Includes a savings deposit log, 52-week challenge, and $1K-in-30-days challenge.

Using the Printable Planner

1

Print on US Letter Paper (8.5" x 11")

Open the PDF files and print at 100% scale. For best results, use a laser printer. The pages are designed with clean margins for hole-punching and binder use.

2

Print Multiple Copies

Print one Monthly Budget Sheet, Income Log, and Expense Tracker per month (12 copies each for the year). Print 1 copy each of the Savings Tracker, Debt Payoff Planner, and Financial Goal Planner.

3

Organize in a Binder

Use a 3-ring binder with tabbed dividers. Suggested sections: Monthly Budgets, Income, Expenses, Savings/Debt, Goals. Add new monthly pages at the front so your most recent data is always first.

Pro Tips for Freelancer Finances

  • Log income immediately -- Do not wait until the end of the month. Record each payment the day it arrives.
  • Set aside taxes first -- Move 25-30% of every payment to a separate savings account before spending anything.
  • Review weekly -- Spend 15 minutes every Sunday reviewing your Dashboard and expense tracker.
  • Track deductible expenses -- Mark every business expense as tax deductible. This saves you money at tax time.
  • Build a 3-month buffer -- Irregular income means some months are lean. Your emergency fund is your safety net.
  • Pay quarterly taxes -- Use the Tax Reserve Calculator to avoid a surprise bill in April. Due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15.
  • Separate business and personal accounts -- This makes tax time dramatically easier.
